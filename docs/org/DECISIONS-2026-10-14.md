# Who we talk to, and what each surface does

**Date:** 2026-10-14 · **Decided by:** Zev · **Canon:** `architecture/ARCHITECTURE.md`

## Decisions

1. **Morgan is the primary ICP:** the owner of a small agency that already has clients. The in-house practitioner and the portfolio builder are secondaries, switched on later. Writing for indie hackers and engineers is the trap: they build it themselves instead of buying.
2. **YouTube is the base,** then the newsletter, then the community. Zev's voice is the moat; the skills carry to every platform even if the channel stalls.
3. **The weekly newsletter stays a tutorial** and gains three short parts: what it did for us, sell it to your clients, and a weekly scoreboard. Systems we run come first.
4. **Local service businesses are SEOPage's buyers,** reached by ads and outreach, never by YouTube. SEOPage sells a page once, or a monthly plan ($149 self-serve, $999 managed with AI support, $1,499 managed with a real person).
5. **seopage.com is the client's door** (buy, brief, connect Search Console, GA4 and Bing, approve, report, support). **os.esy.com is ESY LLC's room.** **api.esy.com holds every record and does the work.**
6. **Monthly SEOPage buyers are Clients** of ESY LLC, sold through SEOPage, with a plan; one-off page buyers are orders in SEOPage's data. This amends api.esy.com `docs/CLIENTS.md` (pending, with Zev's go-ahead).

## Why

- Morgan pays for systems and setups; the existing-owner lane is open while the "start an AI agency" lane is crowded with beginners.
- The proof is the tilt: our own clients' numbers, shown every week, are what nobody else has.
- A vertical that keeps its own copy of clients and connections would fork Esy; a vertical that is only a door keeps one record that os.esy.com, the client and the newsletter all read.
